What is a country?

A country is commonly understood as a political and geographical community associated with a territory, population, and governing institutions. In international law, questions of statehood and recognition can be complicated. Some places exercise substantial self-government but have disputed or limited international recognition. As a result, different organizations may use different lists of countries and territories.

Important: This directory uses the common 195-country educational convention. It does not imply that every country or territory has the same international status, and it does not include every dependent territory or disputed area as a separate country.

2. The Seven Continents

Earth's major continental regions.

🌏 Asia

Asia is the largest continent by land area and population. It includes regions commonly described as East Asia, Southeast Asia, South Asia, Central Asia, and Western Asia. Its landscapes range from Arctic coastlines and vast deserts to tropical forests and the world's highest mountains.

Asia has been home to major historical civilizations, including those associated with China, the Indus Valley, Mesopotamia, Persia, and many Southeast Asian societies.

🌍 Africa

Africa contains immense ecological and cultural diversity, including the Sahara, the Sahel, tropical rainforests, highlands, savannas, and extensive coastlines.

The continent is central to the history of humanity. Its societies include ancient Egypt, Nubian kingdoms, Aksum, the Mali Empire, Great Zimbabwe, and countless other communities with varied political and cultural histories.

🌏 Europe

Europe is a geographically diverse region connected to Asia across a broad continental landmass. Its history includes ancient Greek and Roman societies, medieval kingdoms, religious transformations, industrialization, imperial expansion, and major political revolutions.

Modern Europe contains a variety of national governments and international institutions, including the European Union.

🌎 North America

North America extends from Arctic regions to tropical areas. It contains large mountain systems, plains, forests, deserts, freshwater lakes, and diverse coastlines.

Its history includes Indigenous civilizations and nations, European colonization, the development of the United States and Canada, Mexico's long history, Caribbean societies, and many movements for independence and civil rights.

🌎 South America

South America includes the Andes, Amazon Basin, grasslands, deserts, high plateaus, and Atlantic and Pacific coastlines. The Amazon rainforest is one of the world's most important ecosystems.

The continent's history includes Indigenous civilizations, Spanish and Portuguese colonization, independence movements, the rise of modern republics, and continuing struggles over inequality, resources, and environmental protection.

🌏 Australia

Australia is both a country and the name commonly used for a continent in the seven-continent model. Its environments include deserts, tropical forests, grasslands, and coastal ecosystems.

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have extraordinarily long and diverse histories on the continent. European colonization beginning in 1788 profoundly transformed Indigenous societies and the development of the modern Australian state.

🧊 Antarctica

Antarctica surrounds the South Pole and is covered largely by a massive ice sheet. It has no permanent Indigenous population or sovereign national government. Research stations operated by different countries support scientific study.

The Antarctic Treaty System governs important aspects of international cooperation and the peaceful use of the continent.

4. World History

A broad timeline of major developments in human history.

Prehistory — before written records

Human history extends far beyond the period covered by written documents. Archaeology, genetics, fossils, tools, art, and environmental evidence help researchers study human evolution, migration, settlement, and adaptation. Modern humans evolved in Africa and eventually populated nearly every habitable region of Earth.

Early agriculture and permanent settlements

In several regions, people developed agriculture and animal domestication independently or through the spread of ideas and practices. Farming supported larger and more permanent settlements, but its effects varied. Communities continued to depend on hunting, gathering, fishing, herding, and other ways of living.

Ancient civilizations

Ancient civilizations developed in regions including Mesopotamia, the Nile Valley, the Indus Valley, China, the Mediterranean, the Americas, and elsewhere. Their achievements included urban planning, writing systems, legal traditions, mathematics, astronomy, architecture, trade, and varied political institutions.

Classical and interconnected worlds

Empires, kingdoms, and trading networks connected distant communities. The Roman Empire, Han China, South Asian states, Persian empires, African kingdoms, and many American societies developed different systems of governance, commerce, religion, and knowledge.

Medieval periods

The term "Middle Ages" usually refers to a period in European history, not a single worldwide era. During overlapping centuries, Islamic societies supported extensive scholarship and trade; African kingdoms developed powerful commercial networks; Asian empires expanded; and Indigenous societies across the Americas built complex cities and political systems.

Maritime expansion and colonialism

From the fifteenth century onward, European maritime expansion transformed global connections. Colonization brought conquest, forced labor, enslavement, religious change, disease, resource extraction, migration, and resistance. Indigenous peoples and colonized societies were active historical participants, not simply passive recipients of change.

Industrial Revolution

Beginning in Britain in the eighteenth century and spreading in different forms, industrialization transformed manufacturing, transportation, energy use, urban life, labor, and economic organization. These changes created new wealth and technologies while also producing harsh working conditions, pollution, inequality, and major social conflicts.

World War I — 1914–1918

World War I involved major powers and their allies in a conflict shaped by imperial rivalries, nationalism, military planning, and political crises. The war caused immense casualties and helped transform European empires, international politics, and revolutionary movements.

World War II — 1939–1945

World War II was a global conflict involving Axis and Allied powers. It included aggressive wars, occupation, mass atrocities, the Holocaust, forced displacement, strategic bombing, and the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. The war reshaped borders, institutions, international law, and the balance of global power.

The Cold War

After World War II, the United States and the Soviet Union became rival superpowers. Their competition shaped alliances, nuclear arms development, proxy wars, intelligence operations, technological competition, and political struggles across many regions. The Soviet Union dissolved in 1991.

Decolonization

During the twentieth century, many countries in Asia, Africa, the Caribbean, and elsewhere gained independence from colonial powers. Independence movements used diverse strategies, including political organizing, negotiations, mass protests, and armed struggle. New states then faced difficult questions about borders, institutions, economic development, and national identity.

Contemporary world

The contemporary period is shaped by globalization, digital communication, international trade, migration, climate change, public-health challenges, geopolitical competition, scientific innovation, and debates over democracy, human rights, inequality, and sustainable development.

5. Government and International Relations

How countries organize power and cooperate across borders.

Republic

A republic generally has a head of state who is not a hereditary monarch. Republics differ greatly in their constitutions, electoral systems, protections for civil liberties, and actual distribution of power.

Constitutional monarchy

A constitutional monarchy retains a monarch as head of state while governmental powers are defined and limited by a constitution, laws, and political institutions. The monarch's practical role varies significantly from country to country.

Parliamentary government

In a parliamentary system, the government usually depends on the confidence of the elected legislature. A prime minister typically leads the government, while a separate head of state may have ceremonial or constitutional responsibilities.

Federal government

A federation constitutionally divides authority between national and regional governments. The powers of states, provinces, or constituent units vary according to the country's constitution.

United Nations

The United Nations is an international organization founded in 1945. Its work includes diplomacy, peace and security, humanitarian coordination, development, and international cooperation.

International law

International law includes treaties, customary international law, and other recognized legal sources governing relations among states and other international actors.

6. Earth's Geography

Oceans, landforms, climate, ecosystems, and natural hazards.

The five oceans

Pacific Ocean

The Pacific is the largest ocean. It contains many island groups and is bordered by regions with active tectonic boundaries, volcanoes, and earthquake zones.

Atlantic Ocean

The Atlantic separates the Americas from Europe and Africa. It has been central to migration, trade, colonial expansion, and global shipping.

Indian Ocean

The Indian Ocean connects eastern Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Australia. Its monsoon patterns have shaped maritime commerce for centuries.

Southern Ocean

The Southern Ocean surrounds Antarctica and is important to global ocean circulation and the planet's climate system.

Arctic Ocean

The Arctic Ocean lies around the North Pole and is bordered by northern parts of North America, Europe, and Asia. Sea ice and Arctic ecosystems are affected by rapid warming.

Major natural systems

Earth's landscapes are shaped by plate tectonics, erosion, weathering, river processes, glaciers, volcanic activity, ocean currents, and climate. These systems interact over different time scales, from sudden earthquakes to the gradual formation of mountain ranges.

Ecosystems include tropical rainforests, temperate forests, grasslands, deserts, wetlands, coral reefs, mangroves, tundra, and polar environments. Each ecosystem supports particular communities of organisms and provides services important to human life, such as food production, water regulation, carbon storage, and habitat.

7. Science and Technology

How evidence and invention change our understanding of the world.

Astronomy

Astronomy studies planets, stars, galaxies, black holes, and the universe. Earth orbits the Sun, which is one star among the enormous number of stars in the Milky Way.

Biology

Biology studies living organisms, their structures, functions, evolution, genetics, and interactions with their environments.

Physics

Physics examines matter, energy, motion, forces, waves, electricity, magnetism, and the fundamental behavior of nature.

Medicine

Medical science studies health and disease and develops ways to prevent, diagnose, and treat illness. Reliable medical decisions depend on evidence and qualified healthcare professionals.

Computing

Computing encompasses algorithms, software, networks, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and the hardware systems used to process information.

Space exploration

Space exploration uses satellites, robotic probes, telescopes, and crewed missions to study Earth, the Moon, other planets, and the wider universe.

8. World Cultures and Human Society

Languages, traditions, arts, beliefs, and everyday life.

Human cultures are dynamic systems of shared and contested meanings, practices, knowledge, and institutions. They influence food, clothing, language, architecture, music, family life, celebrations, and ways of understanding the world. Cultures are not frozen in time: they change through migration, trade, technology, education, political events, and contact with other communities.

Languages

Thousands of languages are spoken worldwide. Languages preserve knowledge and identity, and many are threatened by declining use. Documentation, education, and community initiatives can support language preservation.

Religion and philosophy

Religious and philosophical traditions address questions about meaning, ethics, community, existence, and the nature of reality. Beliefs and practices vary widely both between and within traditions.

Food traditions

Food reflects geography, agriculture, trade, migration, technology, religion, and family customs. Similar ingredients can be prepared in very different ways across cultures.

Music and arts

Music, dance, literature, painting, sculpture, film, and other art forms preserve experiences and express creativity. Artistic traditions often develop through both local innovation and cultural exchange.

Architecture

Buildings and settlements reflect climate, materials, technology, political power, religious practice, trade, and social organization. Architectural heritage provides evidence about how people lived and worked.

Human rights

Human rights are rights and freedoms recognized through international instruments and domestic legal systems. Their interpretation, protection, and enforcement remain important issues worldwide.
